1About this manual

This manual describes the physical robot: what it is made of, how the parts are connected, how to take it apart and put it back together, and how to find hardware faults. It is written for the person building, repairing or modifying 2PyBot.

Values come from the firmware configuration (firmware/BaseLink/config.h), the CAD sources in hardware/cad/ and the motor manufacturer's drawing. Where a value is an estimate in the source it is marked as such here. Software is covered in the software manual (SW-2PB-003) and operation in the user manual (UM-2PB-001).

CautionDisconnect the battery before opening the robot, unplugging a motor or working on wiring. A hub motor driver keeps its last current command while powered; a robot on the bench with the battery connected can spin a wheel unexpectedly.

3Mechanical construction

Figure 3. Front and rear views.

3.1 Structure

The robot is built around a stack of three decks of 150 mm × 90 mm, 1.6 mm thick, joined by M3 standoffs at the corners. The hub motors hang below the bottom deck on printed mounts; the controller, battery and camera head sit in the body above; the lidar sits on top.

The CAD coordinate frame has its origin at the front left corner of the bottom deck, on its underside. X runs left to right seen from the front, Y from front to back and Z upward.

3.3 Rest position and bumper

When disarmed the robot tips forward and rests on its front bumper at about 60° from vertical. The bumper takes every fall and every self-uprighting start, so it is a wear part (section 14).

4Drive system

4.1 M0601 hub motors

Each wheel is a DDT M0601 direct-drive hub motor with an integrated field-oriented control driver, a 4096 count position sensor and an RS485 interface. The motors are handed: the left and right versions differ, so fit each on its own side.

PropertyValue
Supply18 V nominal (battery voltage)
Torque constant0.75 N·m/A
Speed constant11.1 rpm/V
Position resolution4096 counts per revolution
Current command scale±32767 = ±8 A
Current limit in firmware2.7 A default, 4.4 A maximum (CURRENT)
Rated and no-load speed115 rpm and 200 rpm at 18 V (about 0.61 and 1.05 m/s)
Wheel diameter (drawing)100.7 mm ±1.5 mm; firmware uses a 52 mm loaded radius
Overall width (drawing)65.5 mm ±0.5 mm
Stator mounting19 mm keyed stator with three flats; three M2.5 threaded holes on a 15.2 mm circle, 5 mm deep

At the 2.7 A limit each wheel produces about 2 N·m. Torque mode is the only drive mode used: velocity mode added an internal speed loop that oscillated against the balance controller.

4.2 Motor mounts

Each motor stator is held in a printed fork that bolts to the four motor anchors under the bottom deck. The revision 1 mount in hardware/cad/m0601_mount/rev1/ is the reference design:

  • A keyed 19.45 mm socket, 13 mm deep, matches the stator flats so the stator cannot turn under torque.
  • A 6 mm end plate carries three M2.5 × 10 mm screws into the stator, giving about 3.5 mm thread engagement with 0.5 mm washers.
  • Four captive M3 nuts in side pockets take the M3 × 10 mm screws from the deck anchors.
  • A slot in the web takes a cable tie for strain relief of the motor cable.

The mount is a prototype printed in PETG or ASA with six walls and 50% to 60% infill. It is not load rated; inspect it for cracks around the socket after hard falls.

5Controller (ESP32)

The controller is an ESP32-WROOM module on a perforated board in the middle of the robot. It runs the balance loop, talks to both motors over separate RS485 transceivers, reads the inertial sensors, drives the LED ring, servos and torch, and pairs with the gamepad over Bluetooth. A WROOM module is required: on WROVER modules GPIO 16 and 17 are used by the PSRAM.

Figure 5. Inside the body, seen through the face: the controller board with its blue status LED, the battery behind it, the USB cable to the host and the torch.

5.2 Strapping pins

GPIO 0 and GPIO 2 decide the boot mode of the ESP32. Both must be low or floating low at reset for a normal boot, and GPIO 0 has a pull-down. The firmware resets both to plain outputs and drives them low before the PWM peripheral takes them over, so the zoom servo and torch do not twitch at power-on. Do not add pull-ups or loads that hold these pins high.

5.3 Reset and boot control

The host holds the ESP32 in reset through the USB bridge's control line until its services are running (boot gate). After release the firmware sends zero current to both motors at once and waits for the host's ready message before calibrating. Flashing also uses the bridge's control lines, so the controller is always programmed from the host; no buttons need to be pressed.

6Motor links (RS485)

Each motor has its own half-duplex RS485 bus with its own transceiver, so a fault on one cable cannot block the other wheel.

PropertyValue
SignallingRS485 half duplex, 115200 baud, 8N1
Direction controlTransceiver DE and /RE tied together, driven by the UART RTS signal
Frame10 bytes, CRC-8, one request per motor every 5 ms
Link timeout60 ms without a valid reply disarms the robot

6.1 Wiring practice

  • Use a twisted pair for A and B, and keep the ground reference between transceiver and motor connected.
  • Keep the RS485 pairs away from the motor phase area and the torch supply.
  • Secure the connectors with strain relief: the wheels and body move relative to each other at every fall.
  • The firmware counts lost replies per wheel. A healthy link loses well under one reply per second; repeated losses on one side point to a cable, connector or termination problem on that side.
NoteAs of 4.7.6 the left link loses about three replies per second while the right loses well under one. The firmware tolerates this, but the left cable and connector should be checked.

7Sensors

7.1 Inertial sensor

The ST ISM6HG256X provides acceleration and rotation rate at 960 Hz with ranges of ±4 g and ±2000 dps. It sits on the controller board; pitch uses accelerometer axes Y and Z and gyro axis X. The board must be mounted rigidly: any flex or vibration between the board and the body appears directly as pitch noise. The QMC5883L compass on the same bus is used for diagnostics only, because the hub motors disturb it indoors.

The pitch trim (−1.26° in the compiled defaults) compensates the sensor's mounting angle. If the controller board is remounted, the trim must be measured again.

7.2 Lidar

The Slamtec RPLIDAR C1 is mounted on top, on the axle line and 31 mm right of centre, so its scan plane does not move forward and back when the body pitches. It connects to UART4 of the host at 460800 baud. Keep the optical window clean and free of tape or cables; anything inside the window is seen as an obstacle at a fixed distance. The lidar cannot see objects lower than its 265 mm scan plane, glass or mirrors.

7.3 Depth sensor

The ST VL53L5CX in the face measures an 8 × 8 grid of distances over 45° at 10 Hz. It is on I2C bus 7 of the host at address 0x29, 210 mm above the floor and 40 mm left of centre, tilted down. It covers low obstacles and drop-offs below the lidar plane.

7.4 Camera

The Sony IMX708 wide camera module connects to the host's MIPI CSI port. It is mounted on the pan and zoom servos in the face. The CSI ribbon must move freely through the full pan range without being pinched; route it with a service loop.

The lidar needs 460800 baud, which the A733 UARTs cannot generate from their normal 24 MHz clock. A device-tree overlay moves the UARTs to a 96 MHz clock; if the lidar stops working after an operating system update, check that overlay first.

9LED ring and status light

The ring is 16 WS2812 LEDs in a channel around the lidar, driven from GPIO 15. LED 9 faces forward and the numbering runs clockwise seen from above (settings LEDFRONT 9 and LEDDIR 1). Brightness is limited to 200 of 255 and the ring is refreshed at 50 frames per second.

Figure 6. LED numbering seen from above, and the ring on the robot.

If the ring is remounted or replaced, run the front check (LED test in the console, or LT,2): only the front LED should light. If another LED lights, set LEDFRONT to its number; if a turn animation runs the wrong way, change LEDDIR.

The blue status LED in the power button is driven from GPIO 32 on its own PWM channel and timer, so it keeps animating during blocking calibration.

The ring and servos are supplied from the 5 V rail and must share ground with the ESP32.

  • Servo end stops are set in software. After replacing a servo or its horn, check the full range slowly before driving the camera to the limits.

11Power

2PyBot runs from a protected 5S Li-ion battery, 16.0 V to 21.0 V (18 V nominal). The robot does not measure battery voltage; the 18 V value in the firmware is a configuration estimate only.

ConsumerSupply
Hub motorsBattery voltage
Host computer5 V from a step-down converter on the battery
ControllerThrough the USB cable from the host
LED ring and servos5 V rail, common ground with the controller
NoteWire gauges, fuse and converter part numbers are not recorded in the repository. Measure and record them before modifying the power wiring rather than relying on assumptions.
  • Charge the battery outside the robot with a balance charger, to 4.2 V per cell.
  • Stop driving at about 3.3 V per cell; motor torque drops with voltage and balance margins shrink.
  • Shut the host down cleanly before switching the battery off, so maps and settings being written are not corrupted.
  • When the robot stays on but unused, use Power down in the console. It stops the camera, lidar motor, depth sensor, LED ring, servos and torch, and disconnects the gamepad, which removes most of the heat. The host stays on so that it can be woken from the console.

12Gamepad

Figure 7. The EVOFOX One S gamepad.

The gamepad (EVOFOX One S) pairs directly with the controller's Bluetooth through Bluepad32, so driving and stopping do not depend on the host or Wi-Fi. Put it in pairing mode by holding HOME + A; in this mode it identifies itself as EVOFOX One S, which is the mode the controller accepts. The controller raises its Bluetooth transmit power to the ESP32 maximum (+9 dBm) for range. To pair a different gamepad, use Re-pair gamepad in the control centre or 2pybot gamepad repair with the robot disarmed. The controller restarts and erases only its Bluetooth storage (NVS namespaces BTstack and bp32); a full flash erase is not needed.

13Assembly and disassembly

CautionSwitch the battery off and wait for all LEDs to go dark before opening the robot. Shut the host down first if it is running.

13.1 Opening the body

  1. Shut the host down, switch the battery off and disconnect it.
  2. Remove the screws holding the shell and lift it off the deck stack. Take care of the LED ring cable and the lidar cable as the top plate comes free.
  3. The face can now be separated to reach the camera, depth sensor, servos and torch.

13.2 Replacing a hub motor

  1. Open the body, unplug the motor cable from its RS485 harness and cut the strain relief tie.
  2. Remove the four M3 screws holding the mount to the bottom deck and take off the motor with its mount.
  3. Remove the three M2.5 screws and pull the stator out of the socket.
  4. Fit the new motor of the same hand: seat the stator flats in the socket, fit the three M2.5 × 10 mm screws with washers, then tighten gently (the thread is 5 mm deep).
  5. Refit the mount, route the cable without contact with the rotating wheel and add a new cable tie.
  6. Check that the wheel turns freely by hand before closing the body.
  7. After power-up, check the System map shows the motor online and in torque mode before arming. A replacement motor must have the same RS485 id (1 left, 2 right).

13.3 Reassembly

  1. Check every connector is seated and every cable clears the wheels, servos and the pan range of the camera.
  2. Slide the shell over the decks, guiding the ring and lidar cables, and fit the screws.
  3. Power up, wait for the start-up loader to finish and check the LED ring front (section 9) and the System map before the first arm.
  4. If the controller board was moved, recalibrate the pitch trim before balancing.

Error code 1 (motor link)Motor cable, connector, transceiver, no battery power to the motorCheck the side shown in the System map; reseat or replace the cable.

Zoom servo or torch flashes at power-onStrapping pin pulled highCheck for external pull-ups on GPIO 0 and GPIO 2.

The motor driver reports these faults: sensor, bus overcurrent, phase overcurrent, stall and overheat. A fault only counts if it repeats in three consecutive replies, so a single corrupt frame never trips the robot.
